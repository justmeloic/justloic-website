Five ways to show the drawings

All five use the real drawings, the site's palette and typography, and light and dark mode (the choice carries across pages). They read from one shared file, art-data.js, and open a piece into the same viewer: it grows out of whatever you clicked, ← → or a swipe steps through, Esc closes.

A · COVER FLOW · THE ONE YOU ASKED FOR

Flip through them like records

The front drawing sits flat while its neighbours swing away in 3D, each with a reflection on the floor. Drag, swipe, use the arrow keys or the scrubber; a flick carries on past where you let go. “Spin” plays through the set by itself.

Trade-off One drawing has the stage at a time, so the page loses its at-a-glance overview, and the three series become marks on a scrubber rather than headings.

B · AFTER HOURS

A gallery wall you light with the cursor

Framed and matted pieces hung salon-style in three rooms. In dark mode the wall is black and your cursor is the torch; the site's theme toggle becomes the light switch. With the lights on, every frame throws its shadow away from the cursor.

Trade-off The work is hidden until found, so it is slower to scan. Touch has no hover: the light drifts from frame to frame and jumps to where you tap.

C · STUDIO DESK

Loose prints you can push around

The sixteen drawings are dealt onto a desk as prints. Pick one up, drag it, throw it and it slides to a stop. Three buttons rearrange everything: Scatter (again for a new deal), By series (three fanned piles) and Tidy (squared up in rows).

Trade-off The most playful and the least orderly: prints overlap until someone moves them, and on a phone a finger on a print drags it instead of scrolling the page.

D · SKETCHBOOK

Turn the pages

A cloth-bound book that opens. Each spread has a typeset caption on the left and a drawing on the right, blended into the page so the scan's paper takes the sketchbook's tone. Click a page or drag it across by hand; series buttons riffle straight to a section.

Trade-off The closest fit for graphite work and the most linear: sixteen turns to see everything. Phones show one page at a time, with the caption under the book.

E · LIVING GRID

Today's page, uncropped and tactile

The same three sections, with the series title pinned in the margin like the years on the timeline. Tiles rise in as you scroll; the one under the cursor leans towards it with a sheen across the paper while the rest dim. A switch compares today's square crop with the full drawing.

Trade-off The smallest change and the safest fit for the existing site, so also the least surprising of the five.

ON THE LIVE PAGE TODAY

The grid crops every drawingTiles are square, but all sixteen scans are 3:4 portrait, so a quarter of each drawing's height is cut off until it is opened. Every preview shows the full sheet; preview E has a switch to compare.

Full screen is a dead endThe current lightbox has no next or previous and does not close on Esc. The shared viewer here adds both, plus swipe on touch.

Edit data once: design-previews/art-data.js. Every preview updates.

The drawings have no titles, years or media on the site, so the previews number them within each series. Add title, year or medium to a piece there and the captions and the viewer pick it up.
